Hazel was known far and wide for her helpfulness. Whenever a friend was in need, she would roll up her tiny feet and rush to help. She loved playing with her friends—like Benny the Bunny, who loved hopping around in the blue skies, and Lila the Ladybug, who painted the flowers in all sorts of colors.

One sunny morning, as the sunbeams danced through the castle windows, Hazel heard a soft, melodic voice wafting through the air. “La la la! A petal a day keeps the frown away!” The sound was coming from the garden outside.

“Hello there, little hedgehog!” Serenade greeted cheerfully. “Would you like to sing with me?”

Hazel thought for a moment. She remembered how brave Benny always was when he tried new things. “You’re right! I want to try!” she declared, puffing out her little chest.

Hazel took a deep breath and began to sing softly, “Twinkle, twinkle, little star…” But as soon as she sang the first note, she became nervous. Her voice quivered like jelly on a plate.

“Oh no! I sound awful!” she cried, covering her face with her tiny paws.

“Don’t give up, Hazel!” Serenade encouraged. “It’s okay to feel afraid. Just remember that practice makes perfect! Why don’t we sing together?”

Hazel looked up and nodded. “Okay! I’ll try again!” 

With Serenade’s comforting voice harmonizing with hers, Hazel felt braver. They sang together under the bright blue sky until they were both giggling and dancing around the garden.

The friends hurried back into the castle, but as they reached the entrance, a loud crack of thunder boomed overhead. Hazel froze in fear. She had always been afraid of thunder!

Benny hopped over to Hazel and said, “It’s just noise! You’re safe here with us.”

“But it’s so loud! What if it never stops?” Hazel whimpered.

Serenade gently swayed her petals and chimed in, “Remember what we practiced? You can sing through your fear! Your song can make the thunder feel smaller.”

Taking a deep breath, Hazel thought about how much she enjoyed singing with Serenade. “Okay! I’ll try!” she squeaked.

With her friends by her side, Hazel began to sing as loud as she could, “The wheels on the bus go round and round…” Her voice rang through the castle like a joyful bell.

When they finished their song, Hazel looked at her friends with shining eyes. “That was amazing! I felt so brave!”

“You see?” Serenade said with a smile. “When we face our fears together, we can overcome anything!”

As the storm passed and sunshine returned, Hazel felt proud of herself for being brave and singing through her fear. From that day on, whenever a storm rolled in or thunder rumbled above, she would gather her friends and sing their favorite songs together.
